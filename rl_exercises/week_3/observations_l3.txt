OBSERVATIONS: REPLICATION OF SUTTON (1988) TD-LAMBDA EXPERIMENTS

This document records the formal observations and analysis of the TD lambda
algorithm implemented on the Bounded Random Walk environment. The primary
objective was to replicate the performance comparisons between temporal-
difference methods and classical supervised learning as presented in Richard
Sutton’s 1988 research.  

    EXPERIMENT 1: REPEATED PRESENTATIONS (Figure 3)

Source Data: experiment1_figure3.csv
Visualization: Sutton_experiment1_figure3.pdf

This experiment evaluated the convergence of the weight vector when training
sets were presented repeatedly until the weight changes became negligible.
It was observed that the Root Mean Square (RMS) error is consistently minimized
at lower values of lambda and increases as lambda approaches the supervised
learning limit of 1.0. Specifically, for lambda = 0.0, the RMS error
converged to 0.1259, whereas the Widrow-Hoff equivalent (lambda = 1.0) resulted
in a significantly higher error of 0.1702. These results indicate that
when learning from limited data in a batch setting, updating based on immediate
subsequent predictions is more effective than updating based on final outcomes
alone.

    EXPERIMENT 2: SINGLE PRESENTATION (Figure 4)

Source Data: experiment2_figure4.csv
Visualization: Sutton_experiment2_figure4.pdf

The second experiment transitioned to a single presentation to evaluate the
learning efficiency and parameter sensitivity under limited data exposure.
The stability profiles observed across different lambda values demonstrate that
lower lambda values (0.0 to 0.3) are highly robust, maintaining low RMS errors
across a broad range of learning rates (alpha). In contrast, higher lambda values,
particularly lambda = 1.0, exhibited high sensitivity. The error diverged sharply
as alpha increased beyond 0.2. These findings confirm that TD methods are more
forgiving regarding hyperparameter selection and more stable under the noise of
individual sequences than supervised learning approaches.  

    SUMMARY OF THE OPTIMAL PERFORMANCE CURVE (Figure 5)

Source Data: experiment2_figure5.csv
Visualization: Sutton_experiment2_figure5.pdf

This analysis consolidated the results by identifying the best possible error
for each lambda when paired with its optimal alpha. The data reveals
a non-linear relationship where the global minimum error occurs at an
intermediate value of lambda = 0.1, yielding an RMS error of 0.0665.
As lambda increased toward 1.0, the best achievable error nearly tripled,
reaching 0.1875. The resulting "dipped" curve is evidence for the TD advantage,
showing this type of learning from a prediction is more efficient than the
Monte Carlo approach of learning only from the final outcome.  

    FINAL CONCLUSION

The replication successfully confirms that for the Bounded Random Walk task,
the TD lambda algorithm is superior to supervised learning in both accuracy
and stability. While the specific numerical results in this simulation differ
slightly from those reported in the paper (probably primarily due to the
stochastic nature of the generated training sequences) the overall findings
and trends match the paper's original conclusions. By utilizing the Markovian
structure of the state space through bootstrapping, the agent achieves
significantly higher accuracy and better data efficiency than an agent relying
solely on end-to-end outcomes.
