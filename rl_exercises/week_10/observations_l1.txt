LEVEL 1: HOW WELL DOES HPO GENERALIZE IN RL?

To run the experiment, we execute
`python rl_exercises/week_10/l1_hpo_generalization.py` (inside the .venv).

Generalization task
-------------------
We generalize across seeds. The environment (a stochastic MarsRover with an
80% action-success probability) and the algorithm (tabular Q-Learning) are
fixed -> only the random seed changes. The seed controls the environment
stochasticity, the exploration and the training trajectory. We optimize the
three hyperparameters (alpha, epsilon, gamma) with Optuna (TPE, 60 trials) on
five TRAINING seeds [0, 1, 2, 3, 4] and then evaluate the very same
configurations on five disjoint HELD-OUT TEST seeds [10, 11, 12, 13, 14].

Expectation
-----------
Since only the seed changes (the underlying MDP stays the same), we expected a
small generalization gap and a high rank correlation between train and test
scores: the configuration that HPO selects on the training seeds should still
be one of the best on the unseen test seeds.

Results (Source data: l1_trials.csv, Visualization: l1_train_vs_test.pdf)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Best configuration on the training seeds:
    alpha = 0.1989, epsilon = 0.1105, gamma = 0.9494

    Train-seed score : 61.55
    Test-seed  score : 60.26
    Generalization gap : 1.29

    Spearman rank correlation (train vs test over all 60 trials): 0.814

Do they match our expectations?
-------------------------------
Yes. The incumbent loses only ~1.3 return (about 2%) when moved to unseen
seeds and the rank correlation of 0.81 confirms that the ranking HPO optimizes
on the training seeds transfers well to the test seeds. In other words, for a
pure across-seed generalization setting HPO configurations generalize well:
Tuning on a handful of seeds is enough to find a configuration that is also
strong on new seeds. The scatter plot shows the trials lying close to the
train = test diagonal, with only the very unstable (high-alpha) configurations
scattering more, because their return depends more strongly on the seed.
