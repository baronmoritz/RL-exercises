Week 6 Level 3: SAC vs. PPO

--- Environment: LunarLander-v3 (Discrete) vs. LunarLanderContinuous-v3 (Continuous) ---
Total steps: 200000
Baselines / Variants tested: SAC (Continuous), PPO Vanilla (Discrete), PPO Enhanced (Discrete)
Number of runs per baseline: 1 (seed: 0) used for direct comparison due to computational constraints
Evaluation interval: 10000 steps

Note on Methodology:
In this comparison, the PPO baselines were trained on the discrete version of the LunarLander environment, while SAC was trained on the continuous version.
SAC inherently requires a continuous action space (using the reparameterization trick on a Gaussian distribution), whereas our PPO implementation was designed for discrete spaces.
Adapting PPO for continuous spaces would have required significant architectural changes that could invalidate earlier test results.
Therefore, we compare discrete PPO with continuous SAC.
While not a perfectly symmetric comparison, the continuous action space is mathematically infinitely larger and generally harder to solve.
Consequently, if SAC performs well here, it only strengthens the claims regarding its efficiency.

Results:
- SAC:
    Starts with moderate performance around 0. It exhibits a significant early spike, reaching approximately +180 at 20,000 steps, followed by a sharp decline
    to around -200 at 50,000 steps. After this drop, SAC stabilizes at a low return between 0 and +20 for the remainder of the training, ending at around -20.
- PPO Vanilla:
    Starts at around -250 and improves steadily and linearly, reaching a final evaluation return of approximately +120 at 200,000 steps.
- PPO Enhanced:
    Starts at around -300, shows a rapid initial improvement, and stabilizes at around +100 to +120 for the second half of training, ending at approximately +120.
Observations (Verifying the SAC Paper Claims):
- Sample Efficiency:
    The paper claims that SAC, as an off-policy method, is highly sample efficient compared to on-policy methods.
    However, in this experiment, SAC does not demonstrate superior sample efficiency.
    It shows unstable learning behavior, with a temporary spike in performance at 20,000 steps, but fails to maintain high returns.
    Both PPO variants, while slower to start, consistently improve and ultimately surpass SAC in final performance.
    PPO Vanilla and Enhanced reach returns of ~120 at 200,000 steps, whereas SAC stabilizes at a much lower return of ~0 to +20.
- Exploration and Maximum Entropy:
    The paper states that the maximum entropy objective incentivizes the policy to "explore more widely, while giving up on clearly unpromising avenues."
    SAC's learning curve reflects this to some extent: the early spike to +180 suggests aggressive exploration, possibly due to high entropy.
    However, the subsequent sharp decline to -200 indicates that SAC struggles to maintain stability in this environment.
    This instability contrasts with the paper's claims of robust exploration. PPO, on the other hand, shows a more conservative but steady improvement,
    suggesting a greedier, more stable exploration strategy.
- Final Performance:
    In this experimental setup, SAC did not outperform PPO. However, because the algorithms were evaluated on different action-space formulations of the task,
    the result should not be interpreted as a direct contradiction of the SAC paper.
- Stability:
    The paper highlights that SAC is very stable. However, in this single-seed experiment, SAC exhibits significant instability, with a dramatic
    spike and subsequent crash in performance. After 50,000 steps, SAC stabilizes but at a low return level. In contrast, both PPO variants show
    remarkable stability and consistent improvement throughout training, without catastrophic drops or high variance.
